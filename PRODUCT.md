# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary: any Victorian resident who lands on the site to check their own address or suburb's public transport score. Most arrive with no statistical background and are not comfortable with jargon; they want a specific, personal answer, not a regional average. Secondary: journalists, policy analysts, and advocacy groups who treat the league table and methodology as a citable, credible source when writing about transport inequality.

## Product Purpose

The site scores Melbourne (and expanding regional) public transport access, address by address and suburb by suburb, and uses that score to make transport inequality undeniable rather than anecdotal. It exists as a policy and advocacy tool first; membership signups fund and sustain that advocacy, but the terminal goal is shifting the public and political debate on transport investment, not maximising signups for their own sake. Success looks like the site being cited, shared, and trusted as a real data source, not just clicked through to a join page.

## Positioning

Fusion costed the fix, not just the problem. Where other commentary stops at "transport here is bad," every suburb and address on this site pairs its score with a real, budgeted network plan that would fix it. That pairing, diagnosis plus costed solution, is the one thing every screen reinforces.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: Join Fusion (membership signup, tiered at Standard/Concession/Free).
- Secondary CTA: share your result, or go deeper via the league table, methodology page, or the costed network plan (`/the-plan`) — lower-commitment engagement for visitors not ready to join.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "My street scored badly, and someone actually costed how to fix it."
- Belief ladder: (1) this score is about my specific address, not a vague regional stat — (2) it's real, sourced from the actual PTV timetable data, and reproducible, not spin — (3) this result isn't natural or inevitable, it's what successive governments chose to fund and not fund — (4) Fusion didn't just diagnose it, they costed an actual network to fix it — (5) joining is affordable and directly funds making that costed fix real.
- Proof on hand: none yet. Pre-launch, there is no press coverage, endorsement, or partner logo to lean on. Credibility has to come entirely from citation (VAGO growth-areas audit, Infrastructure Victoria reports, real GTFS/ABS/Vicmap data) and from numbers a visitor can independently reproduce via the methodology page, not from third-party social proof.

## Brand Personality

Insurgent data journalism: think FiveThirtyEight or ProPublica's investigative data pieces. Confident and evidence-first, willing to be combative toward the political status quo, but never sloppy, shouty, or loose with a claim. The edge comes from rigor withstanding scrutiny, not from volume or outrage. Voice is plain and specific rather than technocratic: written for someone uncomfortable with statistics, not for a policy-wonk audience, even though the underlying methodology is rigorous enough to satisfy one.

## Anti-references

Must not read as a generic government PDF report or civic Tableau-style dashboard, that undercuts the insurgent voice with bureaucratic flatness. Must not read as a generic SaaS product marketing site (gradient-hero, card-grid, corporate-startup visual grammar). Must not read as a major Australian political party site (Labor/Liberal/Greens): the stock-photo, primary-colour, committee-designed look these sites share is the opposite of what this brand should feel like.

## Design Principles

- Every number displayed must be reproducible and citable from the methodology page; credibility is the product, not a persuasion trick layered on top.
- Never show a diagnosis without its costed fix: a score without the network plan behind it is half the argument.
- Default to the address or suburb level, not the regional average; specificity is what makes the score persuasive.
- Earn edge through rigor, not volume: confident and combative in claims, restrained in visual noise.
- Write and visualise for someone who isn't comfortable with statistics; plain language and legible data over policy-wonk density.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA baseline (contrast, keyboard navigation, screen-reader labelling, reduced-motion alternatives for all animation), plus deliberate attention to plain-language copy and legible, non-jargon data visualisation so the score and its stakes land for visitors who aren't comfortable with statistics.
