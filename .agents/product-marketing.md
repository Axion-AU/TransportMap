# Product Marketing Context

*Last updated: 2026-07-01*

## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Axion Ventures is a solo-founder, AI-native venture studio that uses a proprietary AI development methodology (SPARC) to build software ventures at the speed and quality of a small team.

**What it does:** Axion identifies markets where existing solutions are "lazy, extractive, or fundamentally broken," then provides the shared tooling, launch support, and network for contributors to build and spin off ventures as democratic, worker-owned cells — currently Zetto (voice-first Japanese learning app, active), Nexus (financial wellness platform for couples, on hold pending funding), and Aometry (modular, open-source Discord bot platform, active — 46 GitHub stars, 5 contributors). SPARC, the internal AI development framework, is the engine behind all of it and is treated as a venture in its own right.

**Product category:** Venture studio / startup studio (AI-native). Not a SaaS product itself — a builder of software ventures, plus an internal AI dev-methodology (SPARC) that could eventually be a standalone product.

**Product type:** Co-op incubator / federated-co-ops model — Axion is the shared infrastructure, launchpad, and ecosystem (tooling like SPARC, launch support, network), not a top-down equity-hoarding studio. Ventures spin up as democratic, worker-owned cells; Axion takes a minority, non-controlling stake reflecting its infrastructure/tooling contribution, not governance control (see `/approach`, in-progress reframe as of 2026-07-01).

**Business model:** Pre-startup, pre-funding, solo-founder operation. No revenue model yet at the studio level; individual ventures (Zetto, Nexus) will have their own business models once launched (Zetto: freemium with usage-tier limits, no feature paywall).

## Target Audience
Three distinct audiences use this site, which is unusual for a single domain:

1. **Potential co-founders / collaborators** — the primary conversion target right now (see `/collaborate`, `/team`). Not developers — Ethan is explicit he wants people who can do go-to-market, funding, and network-building, not more engineering.
2. **End users of individual ventures** (people who want to learn Japanese via Zetto, couples looking for shared-finance tools via Nexus) — currently light-touch, mostly redirected to external venture sites/waitlists rather than converted on-site.
3. **Prospective investors/partners** — evaluating Axion or a specific venture (Nexus explicitly says "if you're interested in backing it, get in touch").

**Decision-makers:** Individuals deciding whether to volunteer/partner/co-found (no formal buying committee — single founder, single decision-maker on the Axion side).

**Primary use case:** Convince a visitor that this is a serious, credible, early-stage studio worth joining, backing, or watching — not a hobby project or a portfolio filler.

**Jobs to be done:**
- Evaluate whether Axion/its founder is credible enough to co-found or partner with
- Understand what SPARC actually is and whether it's a real methodology or just AI-hype branding
- Check in on a specific venture's status (is Zetto live yet? Is Nexus dead or paused?)

**Use cases:**
- Reading `/collaborate` and submitting the founding-partner form
- Browsing `/ventures` to assess the portfolio and track record (including failures)
- Following a link to zetto.app or the GitHub org for a specific venture
- Reaching out via `/contact` as a generic inquiry (press, curiosity, investors)

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem (external):** Nearly every category of software has an incumbent that got lazy, extractive, or overcomplicated — Axion's stated reason for existing is to fix specific broken products (language apps that reward streaks not speech, couples finance tools built for individuals, food delivery platforms that gouge restaurants and drivers).

**Core problem (internal, studio-level):** Solo-founder studios are inherently under-resourced on go-to-market, funding, and non-technical execution — reflected directly in `/team` ("Not looking for another developer") and `/collaborate."

**Why alternatives fall short (per venture):**
- Language apps (Duolingo-style): reward recognition and streaks, not spoken production
- Banking/budgeting apps: built for individuals, not couples, forcing workarounds and arguments
- Food delivery platforms: commission fees up to 30%, extracting value from restaurants and drivers

**What it costs them:** Users stay stuck with tools that don't fit their actual need (can't speak the language despite a long streak; financial conflict in relationships; restaurants/drivers underpaid).

**Emotional tension:** For the studio-level audience (collaborators/investors), the tension is credibility vs. risk — an honest, still-building operation with two shut-down ventures (MunchRun, MagnetLab) alongside two live ones, asking people to bet on the founder and the methodology rather than a proven business.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct (as a studio):** Other AI-native/solo-founder venture studios and "vibe coding" build-in-public operators — Axion differentiates by pairing SPARC with TDD-first rigor and an explicit "ships real things while others are still writing specs" claim.

**Secondary (per venture):** Duolingo/Babbel (Zetto), Splitwise/Honeydue/joint banking apps (Nexus), DoorDash/Uber Eats/Menulog (MunchRun, archived).

**Indirect:** Traditional dev agencies/consultancies — Axion's pitch is that SPARC lets one founder outperform a hired team ("SPARC made me faster than a team of three").

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- SPARC: a named, TDD-first, multi-agent AI development methodology (not just "we use AI to code") — model-agnostic (Gemini, OpenAI, Anthropic), used to build every venture including this website
- Honesty about failure: MunchRun and MagnetLab pages are genuine post-mortems, not scrubbed from the portfolio — a deliberate credibility play ("Calibrated Effort in Action," "the discipline to walk away")
- Co-op structure, not a top-down studio: ventures spin off as democratic, worker-owned cells; Axion takes a minority, non-controlling stake reflecting its infrastructure/tooling contribution rather than governance control
- Track record of speed: PoliCRM built in 48 hours, Zetto's full spec designed in days, Aometry shipped as a real open-source project (46 stars, 5 contributors, active releases) outside the formal studio process entirely

**How we do it differently:** Build the internal tooling (SPARC) first, treat it as durable IP independent of any single venture's outcome, then apply it venture by venture rather than betting everything on one idea.

**Why that's better:** If a venture fails (as two already have), the methodology and the founder's velocity survive and transfer to the next one — the studio's real asset isn't any one product, it's SPARC + the founder.

**Why customers/collaborators choose us:** Genuine ground-floor equity, transparent read on where each venture actually stands (no "coming soon" vaporware — per the `/ventures` page's explicit stance), and a founder who documents lessons rather than spinning every outcome as a win.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "This is one person with no funding — why would I join or back this?" | Reframe around SPARC as the proven, transferable asset (survived two dead ventures already) and the explicit ground-floor equity offer |
| "Two of your five ventures failed — is this studio actually viable?" | Lean into it: `/culture` already turns MunchRun and MagnetLab into structured "lessons," which reads as maturity, not a weakness to hide |
| "Is 'AI-native' just hype/AI-generated slop?" | Point to concrete technical specifics (TDD-first, multi-agent, model-agnostic, named methodology with a defined process) rather than generic AI marketing language |
| "What do you actually need from me if I'm not a developer?" | `/team` and `/collaborate` already state this directly — go-to-market, funding, network-building, no programming experience required |

**Anti-persona:** People looking for a stable job with a funded team (this is pre-funding, pre-revenue); developers wanting to be hired as an employee rather than a founding collaborator with equity; anyone expecting a live, monetized product to use today (Zetto has no launch date, Nexus is on hold).

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Frustration with bloated dev teams/agencies that move slowly; frustration as an end-user with the specific incumbent each venture targets (Duolingo-style apps, individual-first banking apps, high-commission delivery platforms).

**Pull:** The chance to get in before there's anything to dilute — real equity, real influence, a founder who ships fast and documents everything.

**Habit:** Default assumption that serious startups need a funded team and a polished pitch deck before they're worth joining.

**Anxiety:** Is this founder credible, or is "AI-native venture studio" this decade's version of vague hustle-culture branding? Will the studio still exist in a year? What happens to a collaborator's stake if a venture gets shelved (as Nexus and MunchRun were)?

**Glossary:**

## Brand Voice
**Tone:** Direct, unpolished-on-purpose, confident but self-aware — willing to say a venture "didn't work" or "the economics were also genuinely unworkable" rather than spin it.

**Style:** Short declarative sentences, first-person founder voice on `/about` ("I'm Ethan."), concrete specifics over vague claims (48 hours, 13 years, $500–$1,000 sprint, 50/25/15/10 split) — mirrors the "receipts over sentiment" instinct seen in evidence-forward brands generally.

**Personality:** Blunt, systems-minded, self-critical when warranted, ambitious but currently small-scale and comfortable saying so ("One founder. Looking for the right people.").

**Hard constraints:** Never spin a shelved/failed venture as a hidden win — the site's credibility asset is that it doesn't do this (MunchRun, MagnetLab pages are structured honestly as lessons, not buried).

## Goals
**Primary business goal:** Recruit junior/early-career developer contributors (no equity, no pay) via a two-track program — Track 1: open-source contribution to an Axion repo in exchange for verified "Junior Software Engineer, Axion Ventures" resume credit; Track 2: bring an existing project under the Axion umbrella, keep full ownership, get it listed as a venture on the site plus code review — and separately, funding/backing for Nexus.

**Conversion action:** Reply/DM with stack or project link (Discord-first funnel as of 2026-07-01); `/contact` for general inquiries (press, investors, curious visitors).

**Current metrics:** Zetto — in development, no launch date. Nexus — on hold pending funding, proof of concept complete. SPARC — internal only, not offered externally. Aometry — active open source, 46 stars, 5 contributors. FORGE — mentioned as a Rust/WASM/Docker systems-tooling venture in recruiting materials, not yet documented on-site (verify before publishing). Recruiting program launched on Discord with zero takers so far — constraint diagnosed as distribution (no external reach), not just copy.

**Note on equity:** There is no equity/founding-partner compensation model currently in market — `/collaborate`'s prior "Founding Partner" / "meaningful stake" framing does not reflect the actual program and should not be reused. The real structure: Track 1 contributors get resume credit only; Track 2 contributors keep 100% ownership of their own project. Axion's minority, non-controlling stake language (see Differentiation) applies to ventures spun out of the studio's own initiated projects, not to community recruiting tracks — confirm this distinction before publishing further.
